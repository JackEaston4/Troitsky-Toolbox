\section{Problem Statement}

\subsection{Problem}

The McMaster Troitsky club is a student-run organization that registers and 
supports 4-6 teams of students as they take on the task of modelling, analyzing 
and manufacturing a bridge out of popsicle sticks, white glue and dental floss. 
While the club has been successful in the past, results often vary in performance 
both between years and teams, due to the lack of centralized information and technical 
support for members. As teams progress through several years of competition, 
undergoing material testing, bridge design and analysis, and even experimenting with 
more innovative concepts, skill and knowledge within the club often consolidates within 
more experienced members or single teams, ensuring that when those members graduate, information 
is lost to the club. This project seeks to create a centralized information repository and
technical support system for the McMaster Troitsky club, ensuring information transfer between years and teams, 
and facilitating support and onboarding for new members.

\subsection{Inputs and Outputs}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Inputs: } Range of McMaster Troitsky Club members' and alumni knowledge to build the repository. Past documentation of the club and team 
    specific activities, along with their collected data, will be input into the suitable repositories within the service.

    \item \textbf{Outputs: } Toolbox of services to support the McMaster Troitsky club in information centralization and technical 
    support for members. A simplified process for searching and accessing the range of information the team 
    has collected and will continue to collect.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Stakeholders}

\subsubsection{Primary Stakeholders}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{McMaster Troitsky Club General Members}
    \\
 The general members of the club are primary stakeholders as they will be the main 
users and those that we will focus on supporting with our project. These users will be both supporting and expanding the data the
system provides, and will be the main beneficiaries of the system.
    \item \textbf{McMaster Troitsky Club Executive Team}
    \\
 The McMaster Troitsky Club Executive Team are primary stakeholders as they will benefit from 
both the organizational support the service provides, and the alleviation of the burden of technical 
support and information transfer that is currently placed on them. Their input will be essential
for service design and prioritization of features.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Secondary Stakeholders}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Concordia University's Troitsky Organization}
    \\
 As Concordia University hosts the Troitsky competition, they establish the rules and regulations of the competition, 
and therefore changes what is prioritized by the primary stakeholders. This would influence the design of the system, 
and what features would benefit teams the most.
    \item \textbf{MES - McMaster Engineering Society}
    \\
 The MES organizes engineering clubs and seeks to benefit from the legitimization 
of the McMaster Troitsky Club through a centralized information platform. The MES also provides 
funding for the McMaster Troitsky Club, so the hosting of services and associated costs may be dependent on the given budget.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Tertiary Stakeholders}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{McMaster University}
    \\
 The university itself is a tertiary stakeholder as it benefits from the success of the McMaster Troitsky Club, 
and the success of teams during the Troitsky competition.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Environment}

\subsubsection{Software Environment}
The Troitsky Toolbox will be a web-based application, accessible through any modern web browser. A landing 
for the club will be generally accessible, with services and stored data limited to authorized members.

\section{Goals}

\subsection{Information Centralization and Access}
Create a centralized information repository, allowing users to browse past designs, material testing 
information, and other relevant development information. Users should be able to search and filter through 
information, locating relevant content quickly and efficiently. The system should allow for the addition of 
new information, streamlining the process of growing the repository.

\subsection{Key Model Analysis Features and Bridge Predictions}
Provide a platform for teams to upload their bridge models through a standardized format, allowing the user to 
view and analyze the model, highlighting key metrics and predictions about the bridge's performance. This should decrease 
the barrier to entry to bridge analysis, allowing all members of a team to get involved in the analysis process, even without 
formal modelling knowledge. This will be integrated with the material testing support to provide more accurate 
bridge analysis from the stored material properties.

\subsection{Material Testing Support and Facilitated Result Analysis}
As the McMaster Troitsky club begins to standardize knowledge collection, material testing data 
will be collected and stored in a standardized format. The service will streamline this process, allowing 
testing results to be easily uploaded and analyzed against past results, along with collecting supplementary 
testing information for future years.

\subsection{Simplified and Comprehensive Knowledge Transfer}
Support documentation from the McMaster Troitsky club through exit documents and other knowledge transfer 
methods, ensuring that information and designs are consolidated and accessible to future members. 
Specialized member roles, such as executive positions, will also be able to keep a record of their role responsibilities 
and key factors, to further improve the club organization.

\subsection{Integration of Software Development into the Club}
The maintenance and consistent development of the Troitsky Toolbox will be integrated into the club operations, 
creating a subteam around software operations that will recruit interested relevant members.

\section{Stretch Goals}

\subsection{Preparing for Future Development, Maintenance, and Expansion of Project}
In the process of designing this toolbox, the services will be created with expansion heavily considered. While 
during initial development of services such as the model analysis may be simpler, they will be developed with 
room for a specialized model to be implemented in the future. The project will utilize heavily structured code 
and design patterns, along with thorough documentation to create a solid platform for future updates.

\section{Custom Documents (CDs)}

\subsection{Interviews}
Interviews will be the first form of CD performed, used to gather a large swath of specified and verifiable valuable 
user opinion data. With interviews, more experienced members can have the space to inform the team with as much domain knowledge 
about what the project requires as they are able to, helping to specialize the output to true user needs.

\subsection{Usability Testing}
Due to the nature of this service, the user experience will be vital to the project's success. During the second term, 
the application should be functional and already providing some value to the users, so usability testing will provide maximal value 
allowing the team to assess how the services come together as a whole.

    with certain services being prioritized and others seen more as a bonus feature. At the beginning, the team considered focusing on specialized 
    numerical modelling for the bridge simulation, but felt that the value we could produce lay more in the variety of services described 
    in this document.
\end{enumerate}
