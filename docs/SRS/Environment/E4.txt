\nsection{E.4 Assumptions}

The following properties of the environment are assumed.

\begin{itemize}

\item \textbf{Competition Format Stability:} Despite the product being adaptable to changes within rules and smaller operational changes, 
the overall format of the Troitsky competition is assumed to remain stable across years. While some forms of bridges, materials or scoring 
are revised, the product serves the standard competition format of bridges made only of popsicle sticks, toothpicks, white glue and dental floss, 
loaded at mid-span until failure \cite[Secs.~7.1, 10.5]{TroitskyRulebook2026}. The rule set may change across years, as shown by the summary of changes 
from the previous edition in each rulebook. Competition rulebooks are assumed to be published before each competition, allowing for 
integration into the rule compliance checker once yearly, with further updates as revisions are released.

\item \textbf{User Access and Device Availability:} As the product will be available through a website, there is an assumption that members 
will both have access to a suitable device (desktop or laptop) and a standard internet browser. There is also an assumption that all users will 
have a working internet connection when accessing the service.

\item \textbf{McMaster Troitsky Club Organizational Structure:} The role-based access control of the system will be designed around the 
current club structure, specifically around the roles in the organizational body of the club (general members, team captains, coaches, executives). 
It is assumed that this structure will not change frequently and that the access of each role will remain constant across competition years.

\item \textbf{Continued Club Operation:} There is an assumption that the Troitsky competition, both as a whole and as McMaster's individual 
club, will continue to operate. This includes the assumption that teams will continue to register and that the software subteam will continue to have registered 
members.

\item \textbf{Model File Conventions:} Bridge models are assumed to be exported in the STEP file format. This export will be in line with a standard 
export format outlined by the club, allowing for some consistency in how bridge members and units are represented across files. In line with P.6, these 
file conventions are linked to the most complex and high-risk component of the product, so an agreed-upon standard simplifies the scope of the service, 
serving as a mitigation strategy for the risk.

\item \textbf{Material Behaviour:} There is an assumption of some consistency in material properties while the club purchases materials from the 
same supplier. As the model analysis predictions may rely on the measured material properties of popsicle stick and glue slabs, constant material 
properties are a required assumption to create any form of performance prediction.

\end{itemize}
