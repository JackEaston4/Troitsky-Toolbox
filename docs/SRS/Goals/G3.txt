\nsection{G.3 Expected benefits}
\comment{New processes, or improvement to existing processes, made possible by the project's results.}
%--------------------------------
The main benefit of the Troitsky Toolbox is that the knowledge and support the club depends on no longer rest with a few experienced members. Today, designs, test data, and experience are scattered across teams and personal files, so the level of support varies between teams, and from year to year, and information is lost whenever experienced members graduate. By centralizing this information and providing tools that members of various experience levels can use, the Toolbox replaces these informal processes with consistent ones that persist from one year to the next. The expected benefits for each group of stakeholders are described below.

\subsection*{General Members and Teams}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Faster onboarding:} New members can explore past bridge designs, material test results, and design documentation on their own, rather than relying on experienced members being available to explain them. This lowers the barrier to joining the club and allows new members to contribute to their team sooner.
    \item \textbf{Learning from past designs:} Teams can load past bridge designs into the model viewer and inspect them alongside their recorded results, so that each year's designs build on what previous teams learned instead of starting from scratch.
    \item \textbf{Accessible bridge analysis:} Teams can upload their bridge model and receive an estimate of its failure load and load-to-mass ratio, without needing formal structural modelling knowledge. This allows every member of a team, not only those with analysis experience, to take part in evaluating and improving a design.
    \item \textbf{Early rule compliance checking:} Teams can check their model against the current competition rules before building it, catching violations while they are still easy to fix, rather than after time and materials have been spent on construction.
    \item \textbf{Data-driven material decisions:} Material testing results are recorded in a standard format and compared against previous years, so teams can base their design decisions on the club's accumulated test data, and performance estimates use material properties measured by the club itself.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Club Executives}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Reduced support burden:} Much of the technical support and information sharing that currently falls on executives is replaced by self-service access to the Toolbox, freeing executives to focus on running the club.
    \item \textbf{Reliable leadership transitions:} Exit documents and role records give each incoming executive a written account of their position's responsibilities, contacts, and yearly timeline, so that the club's organization does not depend on informal handovers.
    \item \textbf{Consistency across teams:} Because all teams draw on the same repository, test data, and tools, executives can expect a more consistent baseline of knowledge across the four to six teams the club supports each year.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Troitsky Club as a Whole}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Preserved institutional knowledge:} Knowledge that would previously have left the club with graduating members is retained and remains accessible for future members, allowing the club's capabilities accumulate over time instead of being rebuilt each year.
    \item \textbf{Sustainable software development:} The Toolbox is designed to be maintained by a software subteam within the club, creating a new way for members interested in software to contribute and ensuring the Toolbox continues to serve the club after the original development team has graduated.
    \item \textbf{Improved competitive performance:} Together, better access to past knowledge, analysis before construction, and consistent material data are expected to improve the performance of McMaster's teams at the Troitsky competition, benefiting the club's reputation as well as that of the McMaster Engineering Society and McMaster University.
\end{itemize}
