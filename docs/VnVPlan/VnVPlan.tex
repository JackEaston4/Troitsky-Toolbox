The Software Requirements Specifications shall be verified in the following ways. In case mistakes are found or improvements are suggested, as they inevitably will, corrections will be made and the corrections shall be reviewed again.
The above process is an iterative process and shall only terminate when the portion is deemed satisfactory.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Checklist:} Two checklists shall be developed by the Quality Assurer to verify the requirements, one for each requirement and one for the document as a whole. The former checklist shall be explicitly evaluated on each requirement to determine its quality, and will include qualities like unambiguity, verfiability, traceability, and correct spelling and grammar. The latter shall cover the pitfalls a granular analysis cannot cover, including internal consistency, completeness and documented modifiability.
    \item \textbf{Internal Peer Review:} Each portion of the document, especially each requirement shall be reviewed by a different group member than its author. The whole document shall be reviewed through a group-wide read-through of the document at a meeting.
    \item \textbf{Troitsky Review:} The Troitsky Club is our client and its members are frequently available in order for us to seek their feedback. While showing them the SRS is a fool's errand, all assumptions baked into the document shall be verified by club members through asking if the assumption is true in non-technical language they can easily understand. 
    \item \textbf{External Peer Review:} Other capstone groups will raise issues regarding our SRS through GitHub issues. We shall address those issues, and either incorporate their feedback or dismiss it after determining their suggestion is wrong or not necessary.
    \item \textbf{Instructional Review:} The instructional team, primarily our assigned Teaching Assistant, will look over, comment upon, and grade the SRS. We shall seek to incorporate their feedback into our work.
\end{itemize}

This document shall be verified in the ways outlined below. All errors and weaknesses discovered through this iterative process shall
be fixed and improved, respectively. This process shall only terminate when all errors are fixed and all weaknesses are either fixed or shown to be infeasible to fix.

\subsection{Implementation Verification}

\wss{You should at least point to the tests listed in this document and the unit
  testing plan.}

\wss{In this section you would also give any details of any plans for static
  verification of the implementation.  Potential techniques include code
  walkthroughs, code inspection, static analyzers, etc.}

\wss{The final class presentation in CAS 741 could be used as a code
walkthrough.  There is also a possibility of using the final presentation (in

The implemenation shall be throughly tested to find syntactic errors, semantic errors and weaknesses to be strengthened.
Due to our meticulous development plan, requirements document, and design documents, test-driven development is an excellent choice.
The Quality Assurer shall write these tests, drawing on his colleagues' expertise for guidance. Testing the formalized portions is low-hanging fruit,
and the remaining functions will be demecrated in the design process. Of course, TDD will not catch everything so additional unit tests will be necessary
after the code is written to test more cases and fill in any omissions in the pre-written. The requirements shall play a paramount role in determining the
content of the tests. To verify the fulfillment of the requirements, each functional requirement and each objective non-functional requirement
shall have at least one test verifying it. The remaining non-functional requirements will be qualitatively verified through the Quality Assurer and the Troitsky Club.

The above covers unit testing. Once the unit tests are complete and have all passed, integration and system testing shall begin. When possible, they should
be written with a unit testing framework, the only difference being the test tests multiple systems and is thus an integration test. However,
much of integration testing and virtually all of system testing cannot be verified automatically and must be verified manually through the Quality Assurer
running the program and deeming it satisfactory. The number of parts tested for integration testing shall initially be small and incrementally rise.
The last stage, will be testing the entire system (i.e. system testing).

After system testing succeeds, acceptance testing shall be done by demonstrating the software suite to the Troitsky club.
Once they accept our demonstration, our suite is fit for release.

Testing is an iterative process; failures must be corrected and resubmitted. Any tests under the unit testing framework may 
run automatically. However, all other previous tests must be redone unless the test tests non-critical parts only and that
it is painstakingly obvious that the correction did not impact the test.
