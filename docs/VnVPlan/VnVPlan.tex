\section{General Information}

\subsection{Summary}

The Troitsky Toolbox project seeks to create a centralized information repository and a collection of software
services that provide technical support for the McMaster Troitsky club. These services ensure information transfer 
between years and teams, and facilitate support and onboarding for new members. Served as a web application, 
accessible by all current club members, the services provided will simplify the technical components of the bridge 
design process, decreasing the barrier to becoming a competitive team. 

One of the primary services in the toolbox is the model analysis service, which allows teams to load STEP files of their bridge 
designs and perform basic analysis on the models. This service will provide teams with a simplified analysis of their design, when compared to 
external static model analysis software, and will integrate with the material testing component and the rule compliance checker to provide a more specified analysis given the 
build material properties and current competition rules. Services regarding the information repository will include the ability to search through past designs and load them into 
the model analysis service, allowing teams to upload their designs with exit documentation and providing customized material testing result upload features. A knowledge transfer 
service collects executive handoff documents so that club knowledge carries over between years.
In managing the toolbox access, role-based access control (RBAC) will be integrated to ensure that information is kept within the club, limiting access, and allowing executive members 
the ability to manage club users.

\subsection{Objectives}

The primary objective of the verification and validation plan is to outline the approach to ensure that the Troitsky Toolbox 
project will meet the requirements outlined in the software requirements specification. This plan will help to build 
confidence in the correctness, reliability and safety of the system, focusing verification on high-risk points and safety-critical concerns 
outlined in the hazard analysis.

The V\&V plan will primarily focus on these system objectives outlined below. There are also goals outlined that have been deemed 
out of scope for the current development cycle, but will be outlined in documentation as goals for future year development.

\subsubsection{Primary Objectives}
\begin{itemize}

  \item \textbf{Adequate model analysis correctness:} Testing the correctness of the model analysis service is vital to the usability of the toolbox, as, without 
  ensured correctness, teams will not be able to rely on the results. These results will not only be used by teams in the bridge design process, but will also 
  guide them in making estimates of their bridge's weight and ultimate load capacity.
  
  \item \textbf{Data integrity and persistence:} The system must ensure data integrity and security in the knowledge repository. The repository will 
  serve as the club's main data storage and information transfer, so losing data would be a critical concern and would defeat the project's purpose.
  
  \item \textbf{Access control:} The RBAC in the club will maintain data privacy both between teams and outside of the club. Testing 
  this access control will provide confidence to teams when storing current bridge designs in the system, and ensure that executive and exit documentation is private to the club. 
  
  \item \textbf{Demonstrating functional usability:} The functional usability of the system determines if it will be fully integrated into the club's organization and 
  individual McMaster teams' bridge design and development process.
   
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Out-of-Scope Objectives}
\begin{itemize}
  
  \item \textbf{Industry standard static model analysis performance:} The model analysis service is intentionally designed to provide a simplified approximation of static model 
  analysis, so its accuracy will have a larger tolerance than industry-standard tools and will not aim to match established services.

  \item \textbf{Full year competition testing:} The toolbox testing will not include an end-to-end full-year competition evaluation within the current development cycle. This would require a usable product 
  from the beginning of the school term, allowing teams to use the service throughout their full design process.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Assumptions}
There is an assumption of the correctness of external libraries (BFE.NET, IxMilia.Step, occt-import-js, three.js,
SQL Server) used. It is assumed that they are verified by their maintainers; only our
integration with them is tested.

\subsubsection{SRS - Requirements Elicitation Survey}

In addition to the core interviews for the fall CD, a survey was performed to aid and help justify the requirements elicitation process, specifically with regards to the prioritization of included services within the SRS \citep{TroitskySRS}.

are stored in the project repository, under the interview CD folder.

\paragraph{Member survey} The survey was an anonymous, 11-question Google Forms survey sent through the club's communication channels, collecting responses 
between the 7th and 15th of October 2026, totalling 20 responses. The survey analysis was done in the SRS \citep{TroitskySRS}, reporting on the results and implications from member responses. 
The questions are listed in Appendix~\ref{app:member-survey}.

\subsubsection{Fall - Interviews}

The primary CD for the fall term development is interviews conducted with core stakeholders: experienced Troitsky club members and executives. These semi-structured interviews will 
help outline expectations and prioritization of services from knowledgeable members. These interviews will be anonymized to ensure user privacy and aggregated into usable results for the 
toolbox development process. The interviews will be vital in checking any assumptions made in the SRS with experienced club members.

\paragraph{Interview participants} Focus on more experienced members, who have a better understanding of the club and competition needs. Executives, team captains and coaches 
will be focused as they bring different perspectives tha the services wishes to support.

\paragraph{Role in V\&V} The interviews will primarily validate the decision made in the SRS, helping identity any differences between assumptions made and true user requirements. The interviews will also 
help identify what data is able to be collected, checking with experienced members who have years of personal and team data collected. 

\paragraph{Timeline} A scheduling tool link will be sent out November 3rd, 2026, used to collect possible times for the interviews with the selected members. 
The interviews will be scheduled for before the CD-Fall deadline of 16 November 2026.

\paragraph{Interview format} The interview guide is given in Appendix~\ref{app:interview-guide}.

\subsubsection{Winter - Usability Report}

The CD completed in the winter term will be a usability report, allowing for a more complete assessment of the functional usability of the toolbox. 
This round of testing will be performed with a more complete iteration of the product, allowing for more complete analysis of different system services' usability. 
Users will be gathered spanning different experience levels, both in club experience and technical experience (modelling), creating valuable data about the system's performance. 
Specific user functions will be tested, with tasks that follow the G.5 usage scenarios, measured by completion rate and time on task.

This CD will further strengthen the V\&V of the system, ensuring that complete functional and non-functional requirements come together into a fully usable service.

\paragraph{Participants} Club members with different levels of club and modelling experience, grouped as new members, experienced members, and
executives. For this testing, experience will be separated both at the club and modelling/technical experience level, ensuring that individual bias is taken into account for usability. 

\paragraph{Role in V\&V} The usability test validates the usability requirements, and ensures that core user experiences/features are covered.

\paragraph{Timeline} Testing takes place after the Rev 0 presentations (1--11 February 2027) and before the final demonstrations
(15--21 March 2027).

\paragraph{Test format} The procedure, tasks, and questionnaires are given in Appendix~\ref{app:usability}.

\subsection{Stakeholder Interview Guide}
\label{app:interview-guide}

The Fall CD interviews will be semi-structured, with questions tailored primarily to experienced members, and additionally to specific roles (executives, team captains, and coaches).
The questions outlined below are designed to solidify the requirements elicitation process, and ensure that the SRS requirements are validated against stakeholder needs. Interviews will 
be around 10-15 minutes long, and conducted with 2-3 participants at a time, allowing for both discussion and individual responses. No names will be recorded, only information regarding 
the background of the members, and transcripts will be anonymized before being reported in the repository. The interview guide is intended to be flexible allowing for experienced members to direct 
the discussion to areas they feel are important, while still ensuring that core concerns are addressed. As many of the interviewed members will be a part of many of these user groups, certain interviews will cover 
multiple question sets.

Before each interview, participants give informed consent: they will have purpose of the interview described, informed that participation is voluntary and that they
may skip any question or stop at any time, and focusing on protecting their privacy.

\paragraph{All participants, General Questions}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item What is your role in the club, and how many years have you been involved?
  \item Which tools do your teams use to design, analyze, and store their bridges?
  \item What were the largest challenges you faced when you first joined the club?
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Executives}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item What information regarding the club administration is most important to consolidate?
  \item When you first became an executive, what were your largest difficulties?
  \item How would you like to see the software sub team integrated into the club administration?
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Team captains and experienced members}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item What tool have you found is the most accurate and useful in predicting your bridge's performance?
  \item How close to a model estimate do you expect your bridge's performance to be, including uncertainties? [\texttt{FAILURE\_LOAD\_TOLERANCE}]
  \item What are the most common reasons for bridge failures in your experience?
  \item Does the club have models and recorded failure loads of past bridges that could be used to test the Toolbox?
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Closing, All participants}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Is there anything about the club's information or tools that we did not ask about?
  \item Are there any items that you feel are important to include in the Toolbox that we have not discussed?
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Participants} Participants are grouped by experience level, both in club experience and modelling/technical experience, 
to ensure that individual bias is taken into account for usability. The groups are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item New members (0-1 years in the club) and no modelling experience
  \item Experienced members (2+ years in the club) and some modelling experience
  \item Executives and technically experienced members (2+ years in the club) and modelling experience
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{General Usability Testing Format}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item The participant is given a brief introduction to the toolbox and explained the procedure and purpose of the usability testing,
    and gives informed consent: participation is voluntary, the participant may stop at any time, and no names are recorded.
  \item The participant completes the pre-test questionnaire, gathering user information.
  \item The participant completes the tasks outlined in Table~\ref{tab:usability-tasks}.
  \item After each task, the participant answers the post-task question, recording the time and any additional notes on each task.
  \item After all tasks, the participant completes the post-test questionnaire.
\end{enumerate}

If a second round is run with the same participants, the order of the tasks is changed between rounds to account for
learning effects.
