\section{Workflow Plan}
The workflow process used during the development of Troitsky's Toolbox will be centred around GitHub, and done in sprints which will begin and end at every major milestone. Issues within our KanBan will be the main feature we use to track and assign work.
\subsection{Workflow Overview}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item At the beginning of a sprint all major tasks will be broken down into issues and assigned to group members based on their specialties and interests.
  \item New branches will be made for each issue from the most recent version of the main branch, the issues will be related to their respective branches via the development feature in the issue.
  \item If multiple team members are assign to the same issue they will create sub-issues under said issue and create new branches from the parent issues branch.
  \item When sub-issues are completed they will be merged to the parent branch with a Pull Request (PR), and reviewed by a team member working under the same parent issue.
  \item When issues are completed they will be merged through a PR to main and review by a group member that did not work on said issue, if possible.
  \item When a PR is made it will be rin through the CI/CD pipeline using GitHub actions, and can only be reviewed and merged if it passes all tests.
  \item Once a branch has been reviewed, merged, and passed the CI/CD pipeline it will be deleted.
\end{enumerate}
\subsection{Issue Management}
At the beginning of each sprint, issues and their member assignments will be outlined in our weekly meeting and added to the KanBan as issues by the Current Administrator. It is up to those working on issues to outline their own sub-issues.
Issues will be classified via the tools they relate to and if they are backend or frontend as tags (i.e, 'Rule Compliance Tester', 'Frontend').

\section{Proof of Concept Demonstration Plan}
Due to the nature of the Toolbox providing a variety of services and features, the plan for the Proof of Concept Demonstration has a unique way it approaches the risks presented in the project.

\subsection{Risks}
Since many tools operate independently from each other, or in small groups, the risks presented by a single tool's performace/attainabliity do not directly impact the attainability of another.

With that being said, there is a group of tools and features that have a high level of risk associated with them due to their complexity and connectivity to each other.
These related and complex tools would be uniquely tailored to assist the McMaster Troitsky with visualization, performance estimation, and rule compliance testing of bridge designs.

Since the specific details of rule compliance and performance estimation aren't relevant for the Proof of Concept Demo, the focus will be on extracting and visualizing data from the 3D models of a given bridge design.
The plan to demonstrate the concept of functionality is possible by loading a STEP file (.stp -- universal 3D CAD file format) and displaying a visualization or rendering of the file's contents. 
Along with the visualization, numerical data will be extracted from the .stp file related to the bridge, which eventually will be used to support the competition rule compliance assessment tool and projected bridge performance and metrics tool.
This is the individually most complicated feature that will be implemented in the Troitsky Toolbox, so it serves as one of the greatest risks to the project.

It is also worth noting that although there are many different tools that will need to be appropriately connected within the program, it is not a large risk or feasible to demonstrate during the Proof of Concept Demo.

\subsection{Risk Mitigation}
Due to the nature of the Toolbox, risk mitigation in the specific project context is also unique.
Since the biggest isloated risk comes from an individual tool, the biggest negative impact of that risk (if not successfully demonstrated) is that the Toolbox cannot provide that specific feature.
For this project, a "failed" POC demo would mean that there are a few features (compared to the many planned to implement) that aren't achievable within the scope of the project.
This failure means that the team would modify the scope of the project to include an alternate related feature that bypasses the technical limitation or refine, improve, iterate, and add to alternate functionality of the Toolbox.



\section{Expected Technology}
This project shall be accessible via browser for desktop users. Data shall be stored in a robust database.
The back-end logic shall be rewritten a powerful and easily maintainable programming language, preferably with
a utility which will aid front-end development. The current likely option is the .NET programming
suite, backed by a Microsoft SQL Server database, with the back-end in C\# and the front-end utilizing
Razor (C\# HTML) pages to facilitate rendering web pages. A great advantage of doing so is its 
language integrated query (LINQ) suite, which transfers specialized, elegantly-written code into an efficient SQL query. 

Beside standard libraries, state-of-the-art structual engineering utilities will likely be used to aid calculations,
for implementing complex calculations is prone to error, especially for developers not well-versed in civil engineering.
A likely option is the AutoCAD application programming interface (API) with the Inventor API of AutoDesk Inventor being 
an alternate.

Linting shall be used to ensure production code is free of syntax errors, style issues and bad practices. 
One option is \href{https://github.com/lucaspimentel/cslint}{CS Lint} by Lucas Pimentel, 
freely available as it is licensed under the MIT license.

A unit testing framework shall be implemented to ensure correctness, like the native MS Test, NUnit or xUnit. 
The comprehensiveness of the unit
tests shall be measured in part by code coverage, like the Coverlet tool of XUnit. 
Another tactic to increase comprehensiveness could be to 
correlate the projected frequency of execution of a path with the number of unit tests it has. Such frequency would be determined through
consultation with this project's users.

Continious integration shall be implemented through a distributed version-control system, specifically git on a GitHub-hosted repository.
Each change to production shall be proposed by its developer through a merge request from a feature branch which is exclusively theirs
to the production branch. Barring an extraordinary emergency, the change must be approved by another developer before merging, 
preferably one who is working on a feature which closely interacts with the proposed change. 




\section{Use of AI and LLMs}

This project is not a machine learning project, as it will implement many determinate services for the Troitsky club.
However, utilization of robust AI chatbots (e.g. Claude Code, ChatGPT, Copilot) to write code will increase developer efficiency by decreasing the time each
developer needs to spend drafting and revising code. However, AI is prone to hallucinations and other vulnerabilities. Therefore, each
developer shall throughly review all AI-generated code they generate before creating a pull request and not submit the code for a pull request
until they completely understand the code and are convinced of its validity.

\subsection*{Attendance}

The section describes the rules and expectations outlined for team member attendance.

\subsubsection*{Expectations}

Our team expects near-full attendance at all scheduled meetings, along with the required work for the meetings 
done beforehand. Members should be on time to scheduled meetings, and justification for leaving early must be discussed 
with the other members in advance. While full attendance is the standard, there will be room for members to make 
notice that they will be absent at a future meeting, with the added task of supplying required information before the meeting and catching up on meeting notes after. 
If a member misses 3 pre-scheduled meetings, there must be strong justification, and an additional meeting will be scheduled with the other 
members to accommodate and discuss. Approaching deliverables, meeting attendance becomes more vital and therefore after a single missed scheduled meeting, 
absences must be replaced with a supporting meeting or direct discussion with a member about the meeting contents. For the 
scheduled meetings, in-person attendance is generally expected, but there will be room for virtual attendance when other 
members are notified beforehand. 


\subsubsection*{Acceptable Excuse}

For internal deadlines that the team has set, the expectation is that other members are kept on track of the progression of work and therefore 
made aware that a deadline may be missed before it passes. This will ensure that if necessary, other members may assist in the required work or those 
internal deadlines may be adjusted. For missing meetings or when missed deadlines must be justified, acceptable excuses will range from standard 
academic regulations: sickness, family responsibilities, religious accommodations, and any previously communicated commitments that the team has been made aware 
of. For real project deadlines, there will be no acceptable excuses unless any true emergencies have occurred.

\subsubsection*{In Case of Emergency}

Support for emergency situations will begin with the continuous documentation of ongoing work, allowing for simplified transfer of assignment of tasks.
If an emergency occurs and a team member either will not be able to complete their work or is even unsure of their ability to do so, they must contact and inform 
the team as soon as possible. The rest of the team will then attempt to accommodate and support the member by redistributing their workload, 
ensuring the completion of work that is to the team's standard.

\subsection*{Accountability and Teamwork}

\subsubsection*{Quality} 

The team will expect nothing less than the best effort of each member, seeking to create a final product 
that everyone is proud to be a part of. Each member is looking to not only excel in the class, but also 
function smoothly as a team in the process, so preparation for meetings will be vital. Just like the final 
product, this preparation must be held to a high standard, ensuring that complete team progress is made 
smoothly.

Expectations of effort may be discussed on a per-meeting basis, ensuring that all members are on the same page 
about the work required to achieve the desired final product. As the scope for this project is adjustable, this 
communication between members will be central to keeping expectations in check.

\subsubsection*{Attitude}

Central to the team's success will be remaining respectful, understanding and focusing on collaboration amongst 
one another. A positive attitude is generally expected, but as with any project, members may become stressed, and in those 
situations respect must be maintained, and discussion will be had if something breaks this expectation. 
Each member will also be expected to contribute a fair share of the work, both within the technical development of the project 
and the external tasks required. Conflict resolution will be done in a calm and respectful manner, focusing on maintaining an environment 
that, even through conflict, remains in check. Meetings may be scheduled with the TA and/or professor if issues escalate beyond what is 
feasible for the team to handle.

\subsubsection*{Stay on Track}

Staying on track will be rooted in organization and communication among members, primarily utilizing GitHub task assignment features. Beyond 
that, team members often attend classes together, and capstone progress is always discussed, so there is a constant general understanding of how 
the project develops. The weekly meetings will then formalize these updates, ensuring that all progress is documented. Team and community interactions 
are guided by the code of conduct outlined in the project folder, ensuring that a respectful and considerate environment is always maintained. 
Expectations of members' standards as discussed above have all been a general priority of all the individual members even prior to forming this team, so adherence to these 
expectations is rooted in the group's interest. If a member does happen to miss a meeting or an internal deadline, a coffee or treat should be bought for 
the other members during the next meeting. For more important formal deadlines, any issues that arise from absences or missed submissions will result in 
discussion with the TA and/or professor. 

\subsubsection*{Team Building}

The team will build cohesion through the attendance of Troitsky events and meetings, as the team's members are introduced as formal 
members of the club. These events will provide a more casual place to build a team bond, while also allowing everyone the opportunity to engage 
with the eventual users of the platform, gaining a deeper understanding of the club itself.

\subsubsection*{Decision Making} 

Decisions in the group will be made democratically through a vote, with decisions involving several parameters allowing for a ranking of voting options. 
All split decision handling will begin with more discussion of the topic, where those with more opinion on the subject may 
state their reasoning and appeal to the others. In the case where it remains a tied vote, the supervisor will be consulted and will ultimately
decide.
