\section*{Appendix --- Reflection}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Why is it important to create a development plan prior to starting the
    project?
    \item In your opinion, what are the advantages and disadvantages of using
    CI/CD?
    \item What disagreements did your group have in this deliverable, if any,
    and how did you resolve them?
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Pedro Azevedo}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item The creation of the development plan prior to starting the project is key to establishing the patterns and strategies 
 that the team will use to create clean and maintainable code. Part of the value of this project is its ability to stand without 
 anyone from the current group being a part of it, and central to that will be having well-documented code. The development plan also 
 made us settle on some key factors of the project that may have been decided more on a whim if it wasn't set in stone.

    \item I believe the main benefit of CI/CD is the standardization of builds and submissions, ensuring that what we work on will be properly 
 replicated when built by anyone else, including the TAs and instructors. It ensures that the main branch is always a working version of the 
 service, one that has passed all the checks set up by the team. It brings peace of mind when the project gets busier, and development is moving quicker 
 , ensuring no changes that break the project are made.

    \item There weren't any major disagreements during this deliverable, just different suggestions for technologies for the 
 development plan, which were all decided through some more thorough research. When certain decisions relied on 
 some technical knowledge, the team would often turn to the member who best understood that topic.
\end{enumerate}

\section*{Appendix --- Team Charter}

\subsection*{External Goals}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item The primary external goals related to the benefit and success of this product in 
 improving McMaster's Troitsky teams; fundamentally, we want to have a positive impact on the club as a whole. 
 This project seeks to bring some legitimacy to the club's operations, improving the general quality of life of 
 members and hopefully attracting more interest in the club, both from normal members and, additionally 
 interested software members.

    \item Another external goal would be to have a highly organized and streamlined development process throughout 
 the entire project. With the use of all the GitHub features, the team will lock into an iterative development 
 where all members' input and feedback can be properly assessed and organized to further document our work. The 
 team wants this project to showcase our abilities to collaborate and develop a large-scale project smoothly.

\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Attendance}

The section describes the rules and expectations outlined for team member attendance.

\subsubsection*{Expectations}

Our team expects near-full attendance at all scheduled meetings, along with the required work for the meetings 
done beforehand. Members should be on time to scheduled meetings, and justification for leaving early must be discussed 
with the other members in advance. While full attendance is the standard, there will be room for members to make 
notice that they will be absent at a future meeting, with the added task of supplying required information before the meeting and catching up on meeting notes after. 
If a member misses 3 pre-scheduled meetings, there must be strong justification, and an additional meeting will be scheduled with the other 
members to accommodate and discuss. Approaching deliverables, meeting attendance becomes more vital and therefore after a single missed scheduled meeting, 
absences must be replaced with a supporting meeting or direct discussion with a member about the meeting contents. For the 
scheduled meetings, in-person attendance is generally expected, but there will be room for virtual attendance when other 
members are notified beforehand. 


\subsubsection*{Acceptable Excuse}

For internal deadlines that the team has set, the expectation is that other members are kept on track of the progression of work and therefore 
made aware that a deadline may be missed before it passes. This will ensure that if necessary, other members may assist in the required work or those 
internal deadlines may be adjusted. For missing meetings or when missed deadlines must be justified, acceptable excuses will range from standard 
academic regulations: sickness, family responsibilities, religious accommodations, and any previously communicated commitments that the team has been made aware 
of. For real project deadlines, there will be no acceptable excuses unless any true emergencies have occurred.

\subsubsection*{In Case of Emergency}

Support for emergency situations will begin with the continuous documentation of ongoing work, allowing for simplified transfer of assignment of tasks.
If an emergency occurs and a team member either will not be able to complete their work or is even unsure of their ability to do so, they must contact and inform 
the team as soon as possible. The rest of the team will then attempt to accommodate and support the member by redistributing their workload, 
ensuring the completion of work that is to the team's standard.

\subsection*{Accountability and Teamwork}

\subsubsection*{Quality} 

The team will expect nothing less than the best effort of each member, seeking to create a final product 
that everyone is proud to be a part of. Each member is looking to not only excel in the class, but also 
function smoothly as a team in the process, so preparation for meetings will be vital. Just like the final 
product, this preparation must be held to a high standard, ensuring that complete team progress is made 
smoothly.

Expectations of effort may be discussed on a per-meeting basis, ensuring that all members are on the same page 
about the work required to achieve the desired final product. As the scope for this project is adjustable, this 
communication between members will be central to keeping expectations in check.

\subsubsection*{Attitude}

Central to the team's success will be remaining respectful, understanding and focusing on collaboration amongst 
one another. A positive attitude is generally expected, but as with any project, members may become stressed, and in those 
situations respect must be maintained, and discussion will be had if something breaks this expectation. 
Each member will also be expected to contribute a fair share of the work, both within the technical development of the project 
and the external tasks required. Conflict resolution will be done in a calm and respectful manner, focusing on maintaining an environment 
that, even through conflict, remains in check. Meetings may be scheduled with the TA and/or professor if issues escalate beyond what is 
feasible for the team to handle.

\subsubsection*{Stay on Track}

Staying on track will be rooted in organization and communication among members, primarily utilizing GitHub task assignment features. Beyond 
that, team members are often attending classes together, and capstone progress is always discussed, so there is a constant general understanding of how 
the project develops. The weekly meetings will then formalize these updates, ensuring that all progress is documented. Expectations of members' standards 
as discussed above have all been a general priority of all the individual members even prior to forming this team, so adherence to these 
expectations is rooted in the group's interest. If a member does happen to miss a meeting or an internal deadline, a coffee or treat should be bought for 
the other members during the next meeting. For more important formal deadlines, any issues that arise from absences or missed submissions will result in 
discussion with the TA and/or professor.

\subsubsection*{Team Building}

The team will build cohesion through the attendance of Troitsky events and meetings, as the team's members are introduced as formal 
members of the club. These events will provide a more casual place to build a team bond, while also allowing everyone the opportunity to engage 
with the eventual users of the platform, gaining a deeper understanding of the club itself.

\subsubsection*{Decision Making} 

Decisions in the group will be made democratically through a vote, with decisions involving several parameters allowing for a ranking of voting options. 
All split decision handling will begin with more discussion of the topic, where those with more opinion on the subject may 
state their reasoning and appeal to the others. In the case where it remains a tied vote, the supervisor will be consulted and will ultimately
decide.
